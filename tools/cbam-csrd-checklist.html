Direct CSRD reporting duties now touch a small circle of companies. What hasn't gone away is what your EU buyers ask of you. This checklist walks through the boxes worth ticking before that pressure lands on your desk under time pressure — not to make you "compliant," but to make you ready.

Am I directly in scope?

Since Omnibus I, a non-EU company is only directly caught if it has more than €450m in EU-sourced turnover for two straight years, and an EU subsidiary or branch worth over €200m. Almost no standalone exporter meets this alone.

Likely outcome

Roughly 1,200 non-EU companies remain directly in scope worldwide, down from ~10,000. If this section ticks clean, your real exposure is indirect — through what your buyers ask you. Move to the next section on buyers.

Which of my buyers still matter?

The value-chain cap covered in the next section only ever applies to requests from buyers who are themselves CSRD reporters. List your key EU accounts and mark what you know.

Understand the value-chain cap

Adopted 3 July 2026, built on VSME, generally called the Voluntary Standard. It caps what a CSRD-reporting buyer can require from a supplier with 1,000 or fewer employees — but only for requests tied to that buyer's own CSRD reporting.

Textile exporters, take note: EU fashion and retail buyers were using custom sustainability questionnaires long before CSRD existed. Most of what you're already answering sits under a buyer's private code, not the cap. The skill worth building is telling the two apart, question by question.
