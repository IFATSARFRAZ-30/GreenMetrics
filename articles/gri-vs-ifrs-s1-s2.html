GRI vs IFRS S1/S2

Two frameworks, two different questions — a one-page comparison.

GRI and IFRS S1/S2 are not competing versions of the same standard. They are answers to two different questions: GRI asks what effect a company has on the world; IFRS S1 and S2 ask what effect the world's sustainability and climate risks could have on the company.

The two lenses, side by side

GRI — Impact on the World

Core question
What significant impact does the company have on people, the environment, and the economy?
Materiality test
Impact materiality — significance of the company's effect on people, environment, or economy, regardless of financial consequence.
Written for
Stakeholders broadly — communities, employees, regulators, civil society, and investors.
Example: factory wastewater
Did the ecosystem suffer? Did fish populations drop? Did nearby communities lose access to clean water?

IFRS S1/S2 — Impact on the Company

Core question
Could this issue reasonably affect the company's cash flows, financing, or prospects?
Materiality test
Financial materiality — could this reasonably influence the decisions of investors, lenders, or creditors?
Written for
Investors, lenders, and creditors making capital-allocation decisions.
Example: factory wastewater
Will there be a fine, a lawsuit, or lost customers? Does this touch cash flow or financing?

GRI's three layers

01

Universal Standards

Apply to every company, any industry. Foundational reporting rules, general disclosures, and how materiality is determined.

02

Sector Standards

Written for specific industries (oil & gas, agriculture, etc.). Reflect that different sectors create different impacts.

03

Topic Standards

Individual subjects — energy, water, human rights, anti-corruption. Report only against topics that are material to you.

Where they overlap — and where they don't

Climate change is typically material under both frameworks at once. A factory's effect on a local ecosystem may be significant under GRI with no financial consequence, so it may not clear the bar under IFRS S1. An asset becoming obsolete due to the low-carbon transition can be a real impairment risk under IFRS S1/S2 without necessarily standing out under GRI's impact lens. Same company, same facts — different outcomes depending on the lens.
